## 8. Normal Hand Cricket rules

For each delivery:
1. The batter selects a number from 1 to 6.
2. The bowler selects a number from 1 to 6.
3. Keep the numbers hidden until both players submit.
4. Reveal the selections together.
5. If both numbers are equal, the batter is out.
6. Otherwise, add the batter's number to the batting team's score.
7. Update the scoreboard.
8. Advance to the next delivery.

Examples:
- Batter 4, bowler 2: award 4 runs.
- Batter 6, bowler 6: batter is out.

Only the assigned batter and bowler can submit numbers. Other players can watch the match.

## 9. Baseball Mode rules

Implement these exact custom rules.

### Strike combinations

A strike occurs for these combinations:
- Batter 5 and bowler 3.
- Batter 5 and bowler 4.
- Batter 6 and bowler 1.
- Batter 6 and bowler 5.

For a strike:
- Award zero runs.
- Increase the batter's consecutive strike count by one.
- Show a STRIKE notification.
- Display the count as STRIKES 1/3, STRIKES 2/3, or STRIKES 3/3.
- At three consecutive strikes, the batter is out.

### Matching numbers

If the batter and bowler choose the same number, apply this special rule:
- Add both numbers together.
- Award twice the selected number to the batting team.
- Reset the consecutive strike counter to zero.

Examples:
- 5 and 5: award 10 runs.
- 3 and 3: award 6 runs.
- 6 and 6: award 12 runs.

The matching-number rule takes precedence over any other classification of the delivery.

### All other combinations

If the numbers are different and the combination is not one of the four defined strike combinations:
- Award the batter's chosen number.
- Reset the consecutive strike counter to zero.

Examples:
- Batter 5, bowler 3: strike.
- Batter 6, bowler 5: strike.
- Batter 4, bowler 2: award 4 runs and reset strikes.
- Batter 6, bowler 2: award 6 runs and reset strikes.

The strike counter must belong to the current batter. Reset it when that batter is out and the next batter enters.
